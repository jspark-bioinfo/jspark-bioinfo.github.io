I develop network and quantum algorithms for drug discovery.

I develop computational methods for biology rather than only applying them. My work starts from a simple premise: disease arises from perturbations that spread through molecular networks, and the algorithms we use to trace that spread determine what we can discover. This is why nearly all of my publications introduce new methods, spanning network propagation, graph embedding, network visualisation and machine learning, and applied to problems from drug combinations and adverse drug reactions to inflammatory bowel disease and Long COVID.

I now work on bringing quantum algorithms to network biology, and my ambition is to demonstrate quantum advantage in the life sciences, starting with drug discovery. Biological networks are where that advantage is most likely to appear first: they are large, sparse and highly structured, and dominated by propagation and search problems for which quantum algorithms have known theoretical advantages. My first quantum walk methods have already revealed disease mechanisms in Long COVID that classical random walks missed. I am now building the theory and the experimental validation, in patient-derived organoids, needed to turn results like these into a demonstrable quantum advantage in therapeutic target discovery.
